Social icons stand for social platforms and external social links: sign in with Google or Apple, share, follow us.
The set has 25 platforms in two styles, on a 24 × 24 grid, exported from Figma.

## Styles

### Brand
The platform's own colours: use it where people need to recognise the platform at a glance, such as *Sign in with Google*.

<Canvas of={SocialIconStories.Brand} />

### Gray
One colour, the `icon-base-500` token: use it where several platforms sit together and should stay quiet, such as a footer.
Slack, Zapier, Notion and Dropbox have no Gray version in Figma.

<Canvas of={SocialIconStories.Gray} />

## Dark theme

Brand colours stay the same in both themes. Seven marks are black or near-black in Figma (X, Apple, GitHub, TikTok, Tumblr,
AngelList, Layers) and would disappear on the dark theme, so they turn white there, as the platforms' own guidelines allow.
Snapchat and Notion keep their black outline around a white shape, which reads well on dark.
Figma has no dark version of the Brand set; this is the code's addition. Switch **Theme** in the toolbar to see it.

## Sizes

<Canvas of={SocialIconStories.Sizes} />

Social icons are used in two sizes: 24 × 24 and 20 × 20. Use the size that matches the component and its text.
The Figma page says *three sizes* and then lists two; the two listed are available here.
Icons are square and scale without distortion, like an icon with a locked aspect ratio in Figma.

## Using social icons

- Use social icons only for social platforms. For everything else, use the [Base icons](?path=/docs/foundations-icons--docs).
- Don't recolour Brand icons: the brand colours are part of the logo. For a single colour, use Gray.
- Don't mix Brand and Gray in one group.
- An icon on its own is decorative: screen readers skip it. When it is the only content of a link or button, give the link
  or button a name (*LinkedIn*, *Sign in with Apple*). Use `label` only for a standalone icon that means something by itself.

## Accessibility

- Decorative by default (`aria-hidden`); with `label`, the icon becomes an image with that name.
- Gray icons use `icon-base-500`, a mid grey that keeps at least 3:1 against both the light and dark surfaces, as WCAG asks for graphics.
- Brand colours come from the platforms and are not changed; some (Snapchat white, Layers at 20% opacity) are low-contrast by design.
  Don't make such an icon the only way to understand something.
