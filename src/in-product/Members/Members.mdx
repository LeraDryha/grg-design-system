# Members

A business's team and what each person may do: the owner invites people, gives each one a role, changes it and removes people who left. I designed these screens in Figma for a crypto payments platform; here they are rebuilt from the design system's components, and they work.

**Try it:** press **Add member**, type an address already in the team, then a new one, choose a role and **Send invite**; edit a member's role; delete a member; search by email.

## What the flow has to get right

- **The right access for each person.** Three roles, explained right where the role is chosen, so nobody gets more access than their job needs.
- **Who is in and who is still invited.** A new member waits, in grey, until they accept the invitation.
- **No accidental changes.** Saving waits for a new role, and deleting asks first and says what it means.

## Decided while building it

- **The result is a toast.** Figma closed with a *New member added successfully* modal; a result that needs no answer is a Toast (the Modal rule). The new member appears on top of the list, waiting.
- **The roles note is an Alert** (Info, not filled), with the Figma text in one paragraph; the role names aren't bold, since the Alert's text is plain.
- **The owner is you.** The first row is the account's owner, marked *You*, without edit and delete: Admins can't change ownership.
- **Send invite waits** for a valid address and a role. An address already in the team says so at once; a mistyped one, once the field is left. Both fields are marked required, so Role has no clear button.
- **Save changes waits** until the role is different. The email can't be edited, and its field says so.
- **Waiting for confirmation**, not *Wait confirmation*, on a grey Badge; a waiting member's avatar is grey, as in Figma.
- **A real team's size.** Six people instead of twelve, with invented addresses.
