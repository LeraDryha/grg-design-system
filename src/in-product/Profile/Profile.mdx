# Profile

The person behind the account: their name and password, two-factor authentication, and which notifications reach them. I designed these screens in Figma for a crypto payments platform; here they are rebuilt from the design system's components, and they work. In the app it opens from the avatar in the bar.

**Try it:** **Edit** your name (the avatar's menu follows), **Change** the password. On **Security**, turn on two-factor authentication with Google Authenticator (scan the QR code: it is real) or SMS. Any 6 digits work in this demo; *000000* is a wrong code and *111111* an expired one, to see those states. On **Notifications**, switch Email and In app. On the Dashboard, the avatar's menu opens this page, and switching off *In app* for a kind takes it out of the bell.

## What the page has to get right

- **Safe by default.** Two-factor authentication is one step away and explains what it protects; turning it off asks for a code.
- **Changes that can't be done by accident.** The password changes only through a link sent to the email; the name saves only when it is different.
- **Notifications people choose.** By kind and by channel, taking effect at once.

## Decided while building it

- **The avatar opens a menu:** who you are (name and email; the role sits on the Profile, because in the menu's width it either cut the name or broke the email line), *Profile*, *Company settings* and *Log out* (no sign-out in this demo). The Profile isn't in the sidebar. The bell's settings and *Manage notifications* open the Profile's Notifications.
- **You are the owner** from Members: Guadalupe Schimmel, *Owner*, and the role's tooltip says what that means.
- **Check your email is a step** of the same modal, with the Featured Icon of the *Check your email* example on Modal (not the 3D envelope); *Click to resend* appears after 30 seconds, as in the Input Otp examples.
- **2FA activated is a toast**, not a modal: a result that needs no answer (the Modal rule). It keeps the Figma advice: keep 2FA on.
- **Real QR code and secret.** The QR code is a real authenticator link (otpauth) with a secret made in the browser; an app can scan it, though this demo can't check its codes.
- **Notifications, version 1.** The switches sit in the rows and take effect at once (the Switch rule); version 2 had them in a modal waiting for *Confirm*, which is a Checkbox's job.
- **In app, not Push.** The second switch is what shows in the bell, so it is named so, and it works: a kind switched off doesn't notify in the app.
- **No Payouts row**, and the texts say what happens (*When a deposit arrives*), not *an email notification will be sent* under every switch. *[Company name]* is *My Company*. The 2FA text drops *mass payouts*.
